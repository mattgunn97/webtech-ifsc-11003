I enjoyed the class, as I feel that it gave me a good look at how to build a website from scratch, without going too far into the weeds on the more intricate or difficult topics. My graphic design theory isn't the best, but by utilizing the provided links to Adobe and Google, I feel like I was able to create something that looked passible, although it took most of the semester to find something I liked.

Thoughts of Progressive Enhancment

I think the idea behind Progressive Enhancment is interesting. From what I gathered, in the past people would make their sites and then work on them to be supported on other browsers. Where as with Progressive Enhancment, you support things as you go. You finish your HTML and the content within, without specifically being worried about what browsers or devices can support. It's when you get to the CSS and JavaScript that you actually start to invest time into various ways to ensure the end-user can see the content you're providing. I feel like this is what we accomplished in class all semester: getting the main content formatted and squared away in the standard HTML-5 formatting, then developing the CSS to be accessible to anyone who views it. We only touched on the JS at the end of the semester, so I feel like we didn't get to really explore it, but I think the thoughts are the same on it. Using it hand in hand with the stylesheet gives the user a clean style to view, and not worried about if their device or browser can support what is being shown.
